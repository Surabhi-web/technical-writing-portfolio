Patient Admission and Discharge Procedure

A step-by-step guide for hospital staff to complete patient admission, update records, coordinate discharge, and provide follow-up instructions.

Audience Front-desk and ward staff
Document type Staff procedure
Estimated time 10–15 minutes
Important: Follow the hospital's current clinical, privacy, identification, and emergency-response policies. This topic describes an administrative workflow and does not replace clinical instructions.

1. Before You Begin

Confirm that the admission request has been received and that the patient can be safely registered through the standard admission workflow.

  • Verify that the required admission request or referral is available.
  • Prepare the registration system and required forms.
  • Have the hospital-approved patient identification method ready.
  • Confirm the destination ward, room, or department.

2. Admit the Patient

  1. Verify identity. Ask the patient to provide the required identifiers and compare them with the available registration information.
  2. Open or locate the patient record. Search for an existing record before creating a new one to reduce duplicate records.
  3. Confirm demographic information. Check the patient's name, date of birth, contact information, and other required fields.
  4. Record admission details. Enter the admission date and time, admitting department, attending service, and other required administrative information.
  5. Confirm payment or coverage information. Complete the hospital-approved insurance or billing workflow, when applicable.
  6. Assign the destination. Record the assigned ward, room, or department according to hospital procedure.
  7. Confirm completion. Review the record for missing or inconsistent information before finalising the admission.
Tip: Search for an existing patient record using more than one approved identifier when the system supports it. Avoid creating a duplicate record simply because a search returns no immediate match.

3. During the Patient Stay

Keep administrative information accurate throughout the patient's stay.

4. Prepare for Discharge

Discharge should be initiated only after the appropriate authorised staff have confirmed that the patient is ready for discharge.

  1. Confirm the discharge order or authorised discharge instruction.
  2. Check that required administrative documentation is complete.
  3. Confirm outstanding billing, insurance, or payment steps, if applicable.
  4. Verify that discharge documents are available for the patient.
  5. Confirm the patient's destination and contact information when required.

5. Complete the Discharge

  1. Review the discharge record. Confirm the discharge date, time, destination, and required administrative information.
  2. Provide approved documents. Give the patient the documents and instructions authorised by the care team.
  3. Explain next steps. Direct the patient to the appropriate information about appointments, follow-up services, or contacts.
  4. Update the system. Complete the discharge status and close the administrative workflow according to hospital policy.
  5. Secure patient information. Handle printed and electronic records according to privacy and information-security requirements.
Do not proceed: If required information is missing, the patient's identity cannot be confirmed, or an authorised discharge instruction is unavailable. Escalate the issue to the appropriate hospital staff member.

6. Troubleshooting

IssueWhat to do
Possible duplicate patient record Stop the registration process and follow the hospital's duplicate-record procedure. Do not create another record unless authorised.
Required field is missing Check the source documentation. If the information cannot be confirmed, contact the responsible staff member.
Discharge status cannot be completed Verify that the required authorisation and administrative steps are complete, then escalate if the system still prevents completion.
Patient information appears incorrect Do not overwrite information without verification. Follow the approved correction process.
